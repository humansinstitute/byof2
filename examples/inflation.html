What inflation means

Inflation describes a broad rise in prices over time. The Consumer Price Index tracks changes in a representative basket; your own spending can differ. A fixed sum buys less when prices rise.

Source: RBA, Inflation and its Measurement ↗
Nominal dollars versus real value

Nominal value counts dollars. Real value adjusts for price changes, making purchasing power easier to compare over time. A balance can grow in dollars yet buy less if prices rise faster.

Source: RBA, Australia’s Inflation Target ↗

Calculation: nominal = balance × (1 + interest/100)years; today’s purchasing power = nominal ÷ (1 + inflation/100)years. Zero years preserves the balance. Negative inflation models falling prices; negative interest models a nominal decline. Blank or out-of-range values show a correction instead of a result.
