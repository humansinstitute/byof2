Two plans.
One less mystery.

Bitcoin inheritance connects a legal plan for intended recipients with a practical plan for authorised access.

Who should inherit?

A will records wishes about asset distribution. It is one part of estate planning. Local rules differ, so a solicitor or Public Trustee can help review the legal documents.

Read ASIC Moneysmart’s estate planning guide ↗

Can they recover it?

Legal ownership alone cannot recover a wallet. If nobody knows a recovery plan exists or how to find authorised help, access may be lost. Keep access secrets out of public guides and ordinary family conversations.

Read Bitcoin.org’s wallet security guidance ↗

A fictional family

Meet Alex and Sam

Alex has a Bitcoin wallet. Sam knows a plan exists and where to find the appropriate professional help. A lawyer handles local estate documents; a trusted technical professional helps review recovery arrangements. This example contains no wallet location, access secret, amount or real estate information.

Families may not know a wallet exists or understand recovery. This educational perspective comes from We Are Bitcoin’s inheritance explainer, an educational publisher, not a legal authority.

Three words worth knowing

Executor: the person appointed to administer an estate.

Wallet backup: recovery material that must be protected from loss and unauthorised access.

Private key: secret information used to authorise spending. Never enter it here or share it in a workshop.
